Methods: an agent-swarm model of a weekday in FiDi / SoMa

Engine v0.2 · numbers generated 2026-10-24 22:10 by scripts/build_methods.py from the engine, research/parameters/parameters.json and research/validation/validation.json

1System overview

The Human Project simulates one weekday in San Francisco's Financial District and upper SoMa as a swarm of 14,140 software agents, each standing for \(s = 21\) real people (270,942 people present in the baseline). A run is a fixed point of a MATSim-style loop [1]: agents choose modes on yesterday's congested travel times, cars are loaded onto the OpenStreetMap road graph, link costs are updated with a BPR function, and the day is replayed. After the last replanning day a final day is simulated in full: walking legs, Huff destination choice, walk-by retail, co-presence encounters and the dollar valuation. A plan (closing streets to cars, rezoning, prices, plugin policies) is evaluated by exactly the same procedure as the baseline, with common random numbers, so a plan with no edits reproduces the baseline bit for bit and differences are effects, not sampling noise.

3Synthetic population

Jobs are placed per building from floor area and job densities, then scaled to the LODES control total; residents from dwelling units and household size, scaled to the ACS total. For building \(b\) with office, retail, hotel and other floor areas \(F_b^{\cdot}\) (ft²) and units \(U_b\):

$$J_b^{\mathrm{off}} = (1-v)\,\frac{F_b^{\mathrm{off}}}{a_{\mathrm{off}}},\qquad J_b^{\mathrm{oth}} = \frac{F_b^{\mathrm{ret}}}{a_{\mathrm{ret}}}+\frac{F_b^{\mathrm{hot}}}{a_{\mathrm{hot}}}+\frac{F_b^{\mathrm{cie}}}{a_{\mathrm{cie}}},\qquad R_b = U_b\,h \tag{1}$$ $$J_b \leftarrow J_b\,\frac{J^{\mathrm{LODES}}}{\sum_{b'} J_{b'}},\qquad R_b \leftarrow R_b\,\frac{R^{\mathrm{ACS}}}{\sum_{b'} R_{b'}} \tag{2}$$

with \(v\) the FiDi office vacancy rate and \(a_\cdot\) floor area per job. Buildings without a DataSF record fall back to an OSM-type template. Only part of the job base is in the area on a given weekday: office jobs scale with the Kastle badge-swipe occupancy \(o\), other jobs with an attendance share \(\rho\):

$$W_b = o\,J_b^{\mathrm{off}} + \rho\,J_b^{\mathrm{oth}},\qquad s = \operatorname{round}\!\Big(\textstyle\sum_b W_b \,/\, N^{*}\Big) \tag{3}$$

where \(N^{*}\) is the worker-agent budget. This gives \(\sum_b W_b = 185,770\) present workers and \(s = 21\) people per agent. Agents are allocated to buildings by largest remainder of \(W_b/s\) (deterministic), giving 8,846 worker, 1,770 resident, 2,286 visitor and 1,238 through-traffic agents (14,140 total).

Attributes. Earnings tier is drawn from the LODES WAC earnings split (SE01/02/03 = 6.7% / 8.0% / 85.3%); home region given tier from LODES OD by earnings, with the "other" region shrunk to the share that excludes homes more than 150 km away (head-office reporting artifacts). 4Activity and destination choice

Each present worker takes part in activity \(a\in\{\)morning coffee, lunch, afternoon coffee, errand, evening\(\}\) with a Bernoulli draw of probability \(p_a\) (lunch out: \(p_{\mathrm{lunch}} = 0.52\) [18]); residents and visitors have their own activity sets. The destination of every activity is a Huff / multinomial-logit choice among POIs of the matching categories, with walking distance on the street graph [19]:

$$U_{ij} = \ln\!\big(w_{c(j)}\,q_j\,\alpha_j\big) + b_{\mathrm{cf}}\,\mathbb{1}[j \text{ fronts a car-free edge}] - \lambda\,d_{ij} + \varepsilon_{ij}, \qquad j^{*}_i = \arg\max_{j:\,d_{ij}\le r_a} U_{ij} \tag{4}$$

Here \(w_c\) weights category \(c\) for the purpose, \(\alpha_j\) is the plugin attractiveness multiplier (Eq. 30), \(\lambda\) the distance decay per metre, \(r_a\) a purpose radius and \(\varepsilon_{ij}\) a Gumbel draw from a hash of (agent, POI, purpose), identical across scenarios. Taking the argmax over Gumbel-perturbed utilities is an exact draw from the logit probabilities \(P_{ij} = e^{V_{ij}}/\sum_k e^{V_{ik}}\). POI quality comes from Google rating \(g_j\) and review count \(n_j\) when the POI is matched (388 of 1,225), otherwise from a deterministic draw:

$$q_j = \operatorname{clip}\!\Big(\big(g_j/\bar g\big)^{2}\,\big(\kappa_0 + \kappa_1\ln(1+n_j)\big),\; q_{\min},\, q_{\max}\Big) \tag{5}$$

Walk-by visits. Pedestrians passing a POI's frontage edges \(E_j\) stop with a category capture rate \(\gamma_c\), shaped by an hourly propensity taken from the Google popular-times profile \(g_c(h)\) of that category:

$$\pi_c(h) = \frac{g_c(h)}{\tfrac{1}{16}\sum_{h'=7}^{22} g_c(h')},\qquad I_j(h) = \pi_{c(j)}(h)\,\gamma_{c(j)}\,q_j\,(1+b_{\mathrm{cf}}\mathbb{1}_j^{\mathrm{cf}})\sum_{e\in E_j} f^{\mathrm{ped}}_e(h) \tag{6}$$ $$\text{Revenue}_j = \tau_{c(j)} \sum_h \big(s\,N^{\mathrm{dest}}_j(h) + I_j(h)\big) \tag{7}$$

with \(f^{\mathrm{ped}}_e(h)\) people per hour on edge \(e\), \(N^{\mathrm{dest}}_j(h)\) destination arrivals (agents) and \(\tau_c\) the average ticket. Baseline: 490,161 visits and $10.76M revenue per weekday across 1,225 POIs.

5Mode choice (multinomial logit)

Workers choose among car, transit (BART / Muni), walk and bike with a multinomial logit [23]. Systematic utilities, in minutes \(t\) and dollars \(c\), one way:

$$\begin{aligned} V_i^{\mathrm{car}} &= \mathrm{ASC}_{\mathrm{car}} + \beta_t\big(t^{\mathrm{out}}_{r} + \bar t^{\mathrm{in}}_i + t^{\mathrm{park}}\big) + \beta_{c,i}\,c^{\mathrm{car}}_i\\ V_i^{\mathrm{tr}} &= \beta_t\big(t^{\mathrm{tr,out}}_{r} + \mu\,t^{\mathrm{wait}} + \mu\,t^{\mathrm{acc}}_i\big) + \beta_{c,i}\,\phi_{r}\\ V_i^{\mathrm{walk}} &= \mathrm{ASC}_{\mathrm{walk}} + \beta_t\,\mu\,t^{\mathrm{walk}}_i, \qquad t^{\mathrm{walk}}_i < T_{\mathrm{walk}}\\ V_i^{\mathrm{bike}} &= \mathrm{ASC}_{\mathrm{bike}} + \beta_t\,\mu_{b}\,t^{\mathrm{bike}}_i, \qquad t^{\mathrm{bike}}_i < T_{\mathrm{bike}} \end{aligned} \tag{8}$$

\(\bar t^{\mathrm{in}}_i = \tfrac12(t^{\mathrm{AM}}_i + t^{\mathrm{PM}}_i)\) is the congested in-map car time from the cheapest inlet of the agent's home region (Eq. 13), \(\mu\) the walk/wait time multiplier, \(t^{\mathrm{acc}}_i\) the station-to-office walk and \(\phi_r\) the BART fare. Car is unavailable (\(V=-\infty\)) without a car or without a reachable parking node. The money coefficient converts the time coefficient with the agent's value of time:

$$\beta_{c,i} = \frac{60\,\beta_t}{\mathrm{VOT}\cdot m_{k(i)}},\qquad c^{\mathrm{car}}_i = \tfrac12 c^{\mathrm{park}} + \tfrac12 c^{\mathrm{toll}}_r + c^{\mathrm{voc}}\,(\ell_r + \ell_0) + c^{\mathrm{charge}}_i + c^{\mathrm{plug}}_i \tag{9}$$

where \(m_k\) scales VOT by income tier \(k\) and \(\ell_r\) is the out-of-map mileage. Choice is the argmax of utility plus the agent's persistent Gumbel draw, which is an exact sample from the logit probabilities:

$$m_i = \arg\max_{m}\big(V_i^{m} + \varepsilon_{im}\big),\quad \varepsilon_{im}\sim\mathrm{Gumbel}(0,1) \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; P_i(m) = \frac{e^{V_i^m}}{\sum_{m'} e^{V_i^{m'}}} \tag{10}$$

\(\beta_t\) is set in the engine defaults (it is not one of the sourced research parameters); the cited regional models use work-tour in-vehicle-time coefficients of -0.0134 (MTC TM1) [24] and -0.016 utils/min (SFCTA ActivitySim) [25].

Calibration of the alternative-specific constants

Transit is the reference alternative. During the cold start the ASCs are updated after each block of replanning days by the standard log-ratio correction toward the target worker split \(\sigma^{*}\):

$$\mathrm{ASC}_m \leftarrow \mathrm{ASC}_m + \ln\frac{\sigma^{*}_m}{\hat\sigma_m} - \ln\frac{\sigma^{*}_{\mathrm{tr}}}{\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{tr}}},\qquad m\in\{\mathrm{car},\mathrm{walk},\mathrm{bike}\} \tag{11}$$

Four blocks of 4 days are followed by 10 settling days (26 days in all). The target split is an assumed downtown worker split (car = drive alone + carpool, renormalized without "other" and WFH); it is not a cited value. For context, the two sourced splits it sits between: ACS 2024 SF-County workplace commuters drive alone 42.1%, carpool 8.5%, transit 34.2%, walk 8.6%, bike 3.3% [26]; SFCTA downtown trips (2023) auto 36.9%, transit 20.9%, walk+bike 39.7% [27].

6Network assignment

Cars are assigned per hour of day \(h\) on the directed OSM car graph. Link travel time follows the BPR function [32], with modeled agent volume inflated by the share \(\sigma_{\mathrm{bg}}\) of traffic not represented by agents (taxis/TNC, freight, buses) and capped at \(f_{\max}\):

$$f_e(h) = \min\!\Big(1 + \alpha\Big(\frac{x_e(h)}{(1-\sigma_{\mathrm{bg}})\,C_e}\Big)^{\beta},\; f_{\max}\Big),\qquad C_e = n^{\mathrm{lanes}}_e\,\bar C_{\mathrm{class}(e)},\qquad \tau_e(h) = \tau^{0}_e\, f_e(h)\,\chi_e \tag{12}$$

\(\chi_e\) is the product of plugin edge factors (Eq. 30). A car edge closed in a plan is removed from the graph; parking is restricted to the main strongly connected component of the open graph so every driver can enter and leave.

Inlets and routing. 62 car inlets are generated from every road crossing the map edge. A home region may use a set of inlets \(\mathcal I_r\), each with a head-start cost \(o_{ri}\) (extra outside minutes plus a road-class penalty). One multi-source Dijkstra tree per (region, hour) gives every node its cheapest entry:

$$t^{\mathrm{AM}}_{i} = \min_{k\in\mathcal I_{r(i)}}\big(o_{rk} + \mathrm{SP}_h(k \to p_i)\big),\qquad t^{\mathrm{PM}}_{i} = \min_{k\in\mathcal I_{r(i)}}\big(o_{rk} + \mathrm{SP}_h(p_i \to k)\big) \tag{13}$$

with \(p_i\) the parking node. A virtual super-source links to every entry with its offset, so a closure moves drivers to another inlet. Edge volumes \(x_e(h)\) are accumulated by subtree sums over each tree (no per-trip path walk), each agent carrying \(s/\omega\) vehicles (\(\omega\) persons per commuter car). An inlet toll enters the AM tree as time at VOT.

Replanning and MSA

Day \(k\) re-runs mode choice on the current link factors, loads cars, and averages volumes by the method of successive averages [33]; plans are warm-started from the calibrated state, which counts as the first iterate:

$$\bar{\mathbf x}_{k} = \bar{\mathbf x}_{k-1} + \frac{1}{k+1}\big(\mathbf x_k - \bar{\mathbf x}_{k-1}\big),\qquad \mathbf f_k = \mathrm{BPR}(\bar{\mathbf x}_k) \tag{14}$$ $$\mathrm{gap}_k = \frac{\sum_{h,e}\big|\bar x_{k,he} - \bar x_{k-1,he}\big|}{\sum_{h,e}\bar x_{k-1,he}},\qquad \text{converged} \iff \mathrm{gap}_{K} < 0.03,\quad K = 5 \tag{15}$$

This is a relative change in averaged flows, not a Wardrop relative gap [34]; it measures how much the averaged state still moves. After day \(K\) one more choice + loading pass produces the reported day. Measured per-day gaps (engine run at build time):

Honest reading. Neither run meets the 0.03 criterion at day 5: the baseline ends at 0.0363, the 2nd St plan at 0.0404, and both results carry "converged": false in the API. The gap rises on day 2 because the first day's mode shift is absorbed into the average at weight 1/2. Plans are scored as differences against a baseline settled by the same procedure, which cancels part of the residual movement, but not all of it.

7Traffic evaporation and trip suppression

Removing road capacity does not simply move all traffic elsewhere; part of it disappears [39]. The engine represents this in two ways. Commuter suppression: in a plan, a worker who drives and whose in-map car time rose against the baseline skips the car trip (works from home, combines trips) with probability

$$p^{\mathrm{gone}}_i = \operatorname{clip}\!\Big(-\varepsilon_{T}\,\frac{\max(0,\;\bar t^{\mathrm{in}}_i - \bar t^{\mathrm{in,base}}_i)}{t^{\mathrm{out}}_r + \bar t^{\mathrm{in,base}}_i},\; 0,\; p_{\max}\Big), \qquad \text{gone}_i = \mathbb 1\big[u_i < p^{\mathrm{gone}}_i\big] \tag{16}$$

with \(\varepsilon_T = -0.5\) the short-run elasticity of car trips with respect to travel time [40] and \(u_i\) a fixed per-agent uniform. Through-traffic: each through vehicle compares crossing the area with a freeway bypass:

$$\text{stay}_i \iff \beta_t\,t^{\mathrm{thr}}_i + \beta_{c,i}\,c^{\mathrm{plug}}_i + \varepsilon_{i0} \;>\; \beta_t\big(t^{\mathrm{free}}_i + t^{\mathrm{byp}}\big) + \varepsilon_{i1} \tag{17}$$

and in a plan is additionally suppressed by Eq. 16 with denominator \(t^{\mathrm{thr,base}}_i + t_0\). Validation against the Cairns et al. definition (Δ area traffic ÷ prior traffic on the treated street) for car-free Market St gives −3.26 against a median of −0.11. The model ratio is unstable because its denominator, private-car VMT on the pre-2020 Market St (where forced turns already made through-driving impractical), is small; see Section 12.

8Social ties: co-presence model

Encounters are counted from co-presence. Every destination visit \((i, j, t, d)\) occupies time bins of width \(\Delta\) at POI \(j\). In a bin with \(n_{jb}\) agents (\(N_{jb} = s\,n_{jb}\) people), each person registers at most \(K\) others:

$$E = \sum_{j,b} \tfrac12\,N_{jb}\,\min(N_{jb}-1,\,K) \;+\; E^{\mathrm{street}},\qquad E^{\mathrm{hab}} = \sum_{j,b} \tfrac12\,N_{jb}\min(N_{jb}-1,K)\,\frac{n^{\mathrm{hab}}_{jb}}{n_{jb}} + \theta\,E^{\mathrm{street}} \tag{18}$$

\(n^{\mathrm{hab}}\) counts habitual agents (workers and residents, who repeat their routine). On car-free edges a share \(\delta\) of passers-by lingers and meets others in the same way:

$$E^{\mathrm{street}} = \sum_{h}\sum_{e\,\in\,\mathrm{cf}} \tfrac12\,L_{eh}\,\min\!\big(\max(L_{eh}-1,0),K\big),\qquad L_{eh} = \delta\,f^{\mathrm{ped}}_{e}(h) \tag{19}$$

Repeated co-presence turns into weak ties [42] at rate \(p_{\mathrm{tie}}\) per repeat, with \(R\) repeat days out of \(D\) workdays (time-binned co-location as a proximity proxy follows [43]):

$$\Delta T^{\mathrm{weak}} = E^{\mathrm{hab}}\;p_{\mathrm{tie}}\;\frac{R}{D}\quad[\text{ties/yr}] \tag{20}$$

Neighbouring. Residents' friends on their own street fall with traffic, following Appleyard's San Francisco street study [44] (3.0 / 1.3 / 0.9 friends per resident on light / moderate / heavy streets). With \(A_e\) the daily vehicles on the resident's home edge (agents plus background), \(F(A)\) is piecewise-linear through those three levels at breakpoints \(A_1 $$N^{\mathrm{nbr}} = s\sum_{i\in\mathrm{res}} F\big(A_{e(i)}\big) \tag{21}$$

Baseline: 3,552,723 encounters per weekday, 12,425 new weak ties per year, 98,078 neighbour friendships.

9Economic and valuation model

City Value is the annualized change of a plan against the baseline, itemized by pillar: mobility, social, economy and environment. Health is deliberately not valued: physical-activity benefits (WHO HEAT × VSL) dominated every total, so active minutes and deaths averted are reported as plain metrics only. Crash costs are likewise not monetized. With \(D\) workdays per year:

Mobility is the change in commuter consumer surplus, the logit log-sum converted to dollars [23], computed on physical (bonus-free) walking distances so that a street's amenity is not counted both as time saved and as street life:

$$\mathrm{CS}_i = \frac{1}{-\beta_{c,i}}\,\ln\sum_{m\in\mathcal A_i} e^{V^{\mathrm{w}}_{im}} \quad[\$/\text{trip}] \tag{22}$$ $$\Delta\mathcal V_{\mathrm{mob}} = 2sD\sum_{i\in\mathrm{workers}}\big(\mathrm{CS}^{\mathrm{plan}}_i - \mathrm{CS}^{\mathrm{base}}_i\big) \;-\; sD\,\mathrm{VOT}\sum_{i\in\mathrm{thr}}\frac{\Delta t^{\mathrm{thr}}_i}{60} \;-\; D\,\mathrm{VOT}\,\psi\,\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{bg}}}{1-\sigma_{\mathrm{bg}}}\,\Delta H^{\mathrm{delay}} \tag{23}$$

where a through vehicle that switches to the bypass is charged \(t^{\mathrm{byp}}\), and \(\Delta H^{\mathrm{delay}}\) is the change in agent vehicle-hours of delay, scaled to background traffic with weight \(\psi\).

Environment: CO₂ from in-map VMT (agents + background) and out-of-map commute VMT, at the social cost of carbon:

$$\Delta\mathcal V_{\mathrm{env}} = -D\,\mathrm{SCC}\;\frac{e_{\mathrm{CO_2}}}{1000}\;\Delta\Big(\mathrm{VMT}^{\mathrm{in}}\tfrac{1}{1-\sigma_{\mathrm{bg}}} + \mathrm{VMT}^{\mathrm{out}}\Big) \tag{24}$$

Economy: local value added on the net-new part of revenue, the city's share of sales tax, charge and parking revenue (a transfer to the city) and property tax on new floor area \(\Delta F\):

$$\Delta\mathcal V_{\mathrm{eco}} = D\,\Delta\mathrm{Rev}\,\big(\nu\,\eta + t_{\mathrm{city}}\big) + D\,\Delta\mathrm{Charges} + \Delta F\,P_{\mathrm{assessed}}\,t_{\mathrm{prop}} \tag{25}$$

Social: weak ties and chance encounters at a per-tie and per-encounter value \(\upsilon_{\mathrm{tie}}, \upsilon_{\mathrm{enc}}\), neighbour friendships at half a tie:

$$\Delta\mathcal V_{\mathrm{soc}} = \upsilon_{\mathrm{tie}}\,\Delta T^{\mathrm{weak}} + D\,\upsilon_{\mathrm{enc}}\,\Delta E + \tfrac12\,\upsilon_{\mathrm{tie}}\,\Delta N^{\mathrm{nbr}} \tag{26}$$ $$\mathcal V = \Delta\mathcal V_{\mathrm{mob}} + \Delta\mathcal V_{\mathrm{soc}} + \Delta\mathcal V_{\mathrm{eco}} + \Delta\mathcal V_{\mathrm{env}} \tag{27}$$

Rent pressure is reported beside the total as a trade-off and not monetized. The map client shows the engine's itemized value when it is online; its offline fallback is a simplified toy and is not described here.

10Who wins, who pays: ledger and equity map

The mobility line of Eq. 23 is a sum over people, so it can be split. For a group \(G\) of workers (home region × income tier), matched by agent id between baseline and plan:

$$\Delta\mathcal V_G = 2sD\sum_{i\in G}\Delta\mathrm{CS}_i,\qquad \overline{\Delta\mathcal V}_G = \frac{2D}{|G|}\sum_{i\in G}\Delta\mathrm{CS}_i\quad[\$/\text{yr per person}],\qquad \sum_G \Delta\mathcal V_G = \Delta\mathcal V_{\mathrm{commuters}} \tag{28}$$

Travel time is the time term of the agent's chosen mode in Eq. 8 on physical distances, both legs; money is that mode's out-of-pocket cost from Eq. 9 (car) or the fare (transit), both legs. Both are averaged over agents who travel in both runs. Check: the groups sum to −$1.11M/yr, the engine's commuter consumer-surplus line is −$1.11M/yr. This ledger covers commuters only: through-traffic, background traffic and the non-mobility pillars are not attributed to groups.

11Policy plugin calculus and the Droid verification gate

Besides built-in levers (street closures, rezoning, density, congestion charge, parking price), new policies are Python modules written by the Droid policy compiler. A module declares KIND, PARAMS (with defaults and bounds) and any of three pure-numpy hooks over read-only views of agents, edges and POIs. For active plugins \(p\in\mathcal P\), effects compose as

$$c^{\mathrm{plug}}_i = \sum_{p\in\mathcal P} c_{p,i},\qquad c_{p,i}\in[-100,\,500]\;\$ \;\;\text{(car\_cost\_usd: per one-way car trip)} \tag{29}$$ $$\chi_e = \prod_{p\in\mathcal P}\chi_{p,e},\qquad \alpha_j = \prod_{p\in\mathcal P}\alpha_{p,j},\qquad \chi_{p,e},\alpha_{p,j}\in[0.05,\,20] \tag{30}$$

\(c^{\mathrm{plug}}\) enters the car utility (Eq. 9), the through-traffic stay/bypass choice (Eq. 17) and charges paid; \(\chi\) multiplies car link costs (Eq. 12); \(\alpha\) multiplies Huff attractiveness (Eq. 4). Parameters are clipped to their declared bounds, arrays are checked for shape and finiteness, and out-of-range values abort the scenario. Only modules recorded in approved.json are registered.

Open gaps

  • Muni on Market St: about 0% against +6% observed. With car traffic spread over all 62 inlets, the restricted pre-2020 Market carries too few cars in the counterfactual to slow buses.
  • Cycling on Market St: about +98% against +25%; cycling route choice responds too strongly to car-free streets.
  • Cycling volume: modeled cyclists at the count sites are a small fraction of the 2015–17 counts (ratio in the table); through-cyclists and non-commute cycling are not modeled.
  • Rail volume: arrivals at the three rail inlets are 2–3× BART exits, because each inlet also carries Muni Metro, bus and ferry riders. The hourly shape matches well (r ≥ 0.84).
  • Evaporation ratio: unstable, since its denominator (pre-2020 private-car VMT on Market) is small.
  • Walker vs driver visit frequency: 1.18 against the reported ≈2×.
  • Convergence: the 0.03 MSA criterion is not met after 5 days (Section 6).
