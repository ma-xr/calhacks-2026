Busy streets cost us our neighbors

In 1969 Donald Appleyard mapped who knew whom on three parallel San Francisco streets that differed mainly in traffic: Franklin, Gough and Octavia.

On the light street, residents had three times as many friends, and their ties crossed the street.

Half of US adults report loneliness. The Surgeon General compares the health risk of disconnection to smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day.

When San Francisco debates a street change today, nobody can show who would meet.

Diagram after Appleyard & Lintell (1972); friends per resident from the study. US Surgeon General advisory, 2023.

“Fifty years ago, Donald Appleyard studied three streets right here in San Francisco. Same kind of houses, different traffic. On the quiet street, people had three times as many friends. On the busy one, neighbors barely knew each other.”

“Today half of Americans report loneliness, and yet when a city decides whether to close a street to cars, the hearing argues about parking. Nobody can show who would meet.”

A weekday in downtown San Francisco, one person at a time

  • 270,942 people in FiDi and SoMa, simulated as 14,140 agents with homes, jobs and schedules from Census data
  • 993,552 walking trips, 196,560 transit trips and 177,639 car trips a day
  • Change a street, and every agent replans over five simulated days in under two seconds
  • The engine counts where people cross paths, and which of those encounters become ties

Built from OpenStreetMap, Census LODES and ACS, DataSF, BART station exits and Google Maps popular times collected with Apify.

“CommonGround is a living model of downtown San Francisco. 270,000 people, built from Census and city data, each with a home, a job and a day to get through. Change a street, and the whole city replans.”

Co-presence.Two people in the same café, shop or plaza within 15 minutes notice each other.

Repetition.Regulars who keep crossing paths can become acquaintances, as proximity studies of phones and campuses found.

Calm streets.Residents of quieter streets know more neighbors, using Appleyard's numbers.

Lingering.People stay on car-free streets, so encounters happen on the street itself.

What Droid can touch

  • Writes two files in hp/policies/generated/, nothing else
  • No network: Droid's command sandbox blocks connections, and .env is unreadable
  • One allowed command, the gate. Anything else is cancelled and logged
  • Tool allowlist, a 10-minute limit and at most two fix rounds
  • The panel shows the plan, every tool call, each permission decision, the diff and test output

Where this could mislead people, and what we built against it

RiskWhat we built
A fluent story on top of a modelClaude and Droid may only quote engine numbers, and each answer lists the tool calls behind it.
Synthetic people carry biasAgents are drawn from Census LODES and ACS. Results are split by income third, so the app shows who pays.
One dollar figure gets over-trustedEvery parameter is tagged data, literature or assumed, with its source. Costs, losers and the convergence gap stay on screen.
Social value is the softest numberA weak tie is valued at $1,100 a year, an assumption derived from the UK Social Value Bank, and labeled as one.
AI-written code in a running productDroid works in a sandbox, behind a test gate, and nothing loads until a person approves it.

“A persuasive story on top of a model can mislead a hearing. So no AI here can invent a number, every assumption is labeled with its source, and the people who lose out are always on screen.”

One shared plan for everyone in the room

1

Someone edits

Painting a street calls the set_pedestrian reducer on SpacetimeDB

2

Debounce

Each edit bumps the plan version and arms a 400 ms timer

3

Compute

A scheduled procedure sends the plan to the engine

4

Write once

The result lands in result_summary in one transaction

5

Everyone sees it

Every subscriber gets the same plan, numbers and cursors

1.5 s

from an edit to the pushed result, for a new plan

0.4 s

for a plan the engine has already computed

Edits made during a compute are never lost: when the run finishes it sees the newer version and queues another. Tables: scenario, street_edit, block_edit, player, activity, result_summary.

“Neighbors don't plan alone. SpacetimeDB holds one shared plan: anyone's edit triggers a recompute, and every phone and laptop gets the same result in about a second and a half.”

Measured locally (backend/live/README.md). The app's “Go live” button uses this layer (./run.sh --live); commit 55f85de measured about 3 s from an edit in one browser to the settled result in another.
