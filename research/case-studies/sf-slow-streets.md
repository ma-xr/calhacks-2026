# Case Study: SF Slow Streets (2020–), plus Valencia St center bikeway (2023) and Shared Spaces / parklets

**Relevance to our sim:** These cases calibrate "soft" pedestrianisation: through-traffic restrictions and filtered streets. They give before/after volumes, speeds, collisions, adjacent-street diversion, walk/bike uplift, and the best SF evidence on merchant revenue when curb/street space changes.

---

## Part A: Slow Streets

### A1. Context and intervention

| Item | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Launch | Phases 1–2 implemented **May–June 2020** as a COVID response. Temporary signs and barricades restrict *non-local* through traffic ("soft diversion") | [SFMTA Phase 1&2 summary, Oct 2020](https://www.sfmta.com/sites/default/files/reports-and-documents/2020/10/slow_streets_phase_1_and_2_evaluation_-_summary_of_findings_-_october_2020.pdf) |
| Network size (2021) | ~25+ corridors evaluated | [SFMTA Slow Streets Evaluation, Summer 2021](https://www.sfmta.com/sites/default/files/reports-and-documents/2021/09/slow_street_evaluation_report_final_0.pdf) |
| Permanent program | 16–18 permanent corridors. Board targets adopted Dec 2022: **<1,000 vehicles/day** and **<15 mph** | [SFMTA Slow Streets Evaluation 2023](https://www.sfmta.com/sites/default/files/reports-and-documents/2023/12/2023_slow_streets_evaluation_report_accessible.pdf) |
| Low-stress thresholds used | Max 3,000 ADT / <25 mph. Ideal <1,500 ADT | [SFMTA 2021](https://www.sfmta.com/sites/default/files/reports-and-documents/2021/09/slow_street_evaluation_report_final_0.pdf) |

### A2. Measured before/after

| Metric | 2020 (Phase 1–2, ~1/3 of corridors) | 2021 evaluation (60% of corridors had pre-data) | 2023 evaluation (10 corridors with pre-data) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vehicle volume on Slow Street | **−52% weekday, −54% weekend** | **−35%** avg daily traffic | **−61%** after designation. Program-wide 724 → 851 veh/day 2021→2023 (**+17%** rebound) |
| Vehicle speed | ~−2 mph avg (not significant); medians 18–24 mph | **−14%** typical median speed | **−18%** typical median speed. All corridors ≤20 mph median; only 4 of 16 meet the 15 mph target. <1% of vehicles >30 mph |
| People biking | **+65% weekday, +80% weekend** | **+27%** | n/a |
| People walking | **+17% weekday, −31% weekend**. Walking *in the roadway* +130% weekday | **+65%** | n/a |
| Daily users on network | n/a | ~35,000 walk or bike on Slow Streets on a typical day | n/a |
| Collisions | n/a | **−36%** | **−48%** (monthly rate per mile 0.50 → 0.26) vs **−14% citywide** (0.64 → 0.56) |
| Adjacent-street diversion | "Slight increases… not proportional to the decrease" on Slow Streets | Avg hourly V/C on adjacent streets **0.23**, avg max V/C **0.35** (moderate congestion = 0.5–0.75). 1 of 25 corridors showed moderate congestion; 5 close | n/a |
| Public support | Lake St residents: 73% support | 71% agree street is safer; 77% notice less traffic | n/a |

Sources: [Oct 2020 summary](https://www.sfmta.com/sites/default/files/reports-and-documents/2020/10/slow_streets_phase_1_and_2_evaluation_-_summary_of_findings_-_october_2020.pdf); [Summer 2021 report](https://www.sfmta.com/sites/default/files/reports-and-documents/2021/09/slow_street_evaluation_report_final_0.pdf); [2023 report](https://www.sfmta.com/sites/default/files/reports-and-documents/2023/12/2023_slow_streets_evaluation_report_accessible.pdf); [SFMTA blog, May 2023](https://www.sfmta.com/blog/traffic-collisions-have-decreased-san-franciscos-slow-streets).

2023 per-corridor ADT examples ([2023 report](https://www.sfmta.com/sites/default/files/reports-and-documents/2023/12/2023_slow_streets_evaluation_report_accessible.pdf)): Sanchez 320, Cabrillo 370, Hearst 460, Lyon 480, Somerset 490, Clay 550, 23rd Ave 600, Shotwell 600, 12th Ave 700, Golden Gate Ave 790, Lake 820, Arlington 900, Minnesota 1,090, Page 1,120, Noe 1,690, 20th St 2,240.

### A3. Caveats

COVID changed travel citywide, especially in 2020. The weekend walking decline in 2020 likely reflects the lockdown. Diversion analysis uses rough V/C estimates (SFMTA's own caveat). Pre-implementation data exists for only a subset of corridors. Volumes rebounded 17% between 2021 and 2023 as soft barricades degraded and traffic returned.

## What our model should reproduce

| Target | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle volume on a filtered ("slow") street | **−35% to −61%** (soft diversion). Expect a ~+17% rebound over 2 years without hard filters | SFMTA 2021, 2023 |
| Speed on filtered street | −14% to −18% median | SFMTA 2021, 2023 |
| `ped_footfall_uplift` on filtered residential street | **+17% (weekday, 2020) to +65% (2021)**. Central estimate ~+40% (unverified midpoint, our assumption) | SFMTA 2020, 2021 |
| Cycling uplift on filtered street | +27% to +65% (weekday) | SFMTA 2020, 2021 |
| Collisions on treated street | **−36% to −48%** vs −14% citywide background | SFMTA 2021, 2023 |
| Adjacent-street diversion | Increase "not proportional" to the removed volume. Adjacent V/C stays < 0.5 for 24 of 25 corridors. Implies most removed traffic either reroutes thinly across the grid or evaporates | SFMTA 2020, 2021 |
| `ped_street_retail_uplift` (parklet/outdoor seating) | **+19 pp** relative revenue growth (29% vs 10%) for businesses with parklets, COVID-era. Treat as an upper bound | Controller 2021 via SF Standard |
| Retail under a bike-lane/curb reconfiguration | **~0% net** (Valencia tracked within ±1–4 pp of comparison areas) despite merchant claims of −30% to −50% | Mission Local / Controller data |
| Bike-lane design sensitivity | A badly designed facility can produce ≈0% bike gain (Valencia center-running −2% to +3%) vs ~+50% for a good side-running protected lane | SFCTA 2024; Streetsblog |
