# Case Study: Central Freeway removal and Octavia Boulevard (1989 → 2005)

**Relevance to our sim:** This is the best-measured SF case of *where removed traffic goes*: a license-plate survey plus detour-route counts. It gives explicit diversion vs evaporation shares, plus Hayes Valley property effects. It is outside our bbox, but it is the strongest local calibration for `traffic_evaporation_median` and the diversion split.

## 4. Caveats / confounds

- **Volume baselines differ**: 80k, 90k, 93.1k and 97k. Use 93,100 (1995, SF DPT via Cervero) as the citable baseline.
- The **44,900** figure is the boulevard itself. Network-level diversion was not re-measured in 2005. The 2007 count of 63,000 measures a different screen (freeway on/off at Octavia).
- Survey shares are *stated* behaviour from a mail survey of former users, so non-response bias is likely. The 1996 closure also had heavy "scare-away" media coverage, which Cervero et al. compare to the 1984 LA Olympics effect.
- The 1996→2006 property figures span the dot-com boom. The hedonic model controls for building and neighbourhood attributes but not every market trend.

## 6. What our model should reproduce

| Target | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Treated-corridor volume after conversion to boulevard | **−52%** (93,100 → 44,900 veh/day) | Cervero et al. via SF DPT |
| Diversion split for removed capacity (stated, 1996) | **~76% another freeway or freeway combo, ~11% city streets, ~2–3% transit, ~3% trip dropped**. Plus 20% report *fewer* trips | Use as the split when the model reassigns removed demand. Our FiDi grid has no parallel freeway, so expect the "city streets" share to dominate |
| `traffic_evaporation_median` | Hard evaporation (trip no longer made) ≈ **3%** of users. Partial trip reduction reported by ~20%. Combined with Goodwin's ~25% literature median, a defensible SF range for evaporation is **~5–25%** (our synthesis, not a single measured value) | Systan 1997; Goodwin 1998 via Cervero |
| Short-run detour loading | ~42% of removed volume shows up on 3 primary detours within 6 weeks | Robbins et al. 2001 via Cervero |
| Trip length | +7–8% for diverted drivers | Systan 1997 |
| Residual congestion | Boulevard at capacity at peak; adjacent bus delay up to 2.5 min | Model should show *localised* peak delay, not network gridlock |
| Property value | Positive amenity jump (~+$117k/sale, 2007$) concentrated within ¼ mi, decaying with distance. Neighbourhood price ratio vs city 66% → 91% over a decade | Cervero; Garrick & Billings |
| Safety risk | Design-dependent. Freeway ramps meeting a high-bike street created a crash hotspot (≥15 bike crashes in ~3 yrs). The model should penalise high-speed turning conflicts at ramp junctions | City Attorney; KQED |
