# Seoul: Cheonggyecheon elevated-highway removal and stream restoration (2003–2005)

## Context
The Cheonggye stream in central Seoul was covered with concrete from 1958. An elevated highway was built over it in stages from 1969 to 1976 ([en.wikipedia, Cheonggyecheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheonggyecheon)). The elevated road and the surface road beneath carried ~169,000 vehicles/day ([Landscape Architecture Foundation, Landscape Performance Series, Robinson & Hopton 2011, doi:10.31353/cs0140](https://www.landscapeperformance.org/case-study-briefs/cheonggyecheon-stream-restoration)). Repairing the deteriorating structure would have cost ~₩100 bn and 3 years (same source).

## Caveats / confounds
- The −2.3% downtown entry figure means most of the 169,000 veh/day re-routed or changed time/mode, rather than vanishing; "evaporation" is only meaningful relative to the corridor. Removed corridor volume ≫ net downtown reduction. The citywide bus reform (2004) happened at the same time.
- Temperature figures compare the stream with a parallel road, not before/after at the same point. The water is pumped (energy cost) and the stream runs below street level, so the cooling is not transferable to a paved plaza.
- The +30–50% land price figure is LAF's compilation without a counterfactual; Kang & Cervero confirm a premium with a hedonic model, but we did not access their coefficients. The city operator's page reported no clear rent change during construction.
- Displacement of street vendors/merchants and gentrification were contested (not quantified here (unverified)).

## What our model should reproduce
| Parameter / output | Target |
|---|---|
| `traffic_evaporation_median` | Removing a 169k veh/day urban motorway changed downtown entries by only −2.3% with unchanged downtown speeds. Interpret this as: the network adapts (re-routing, retiming, transit) without gridlock. Corridor-level "lost" traffic is large, area-level reduction is small. Use it as a sanity check that a FiDi plan does not create network collapse, not as an evaporation % for a CBD street. |
| `heat_walk_rr` | Blue-green corridor 3.3–5.9 °C cooler than parallel streets. For tree-lined plazas without water, use a smaller cooling (≈1–3 °C (unverified; see literature folder)). Higher walking comfort on hot days should raise `heat_walk_rr` toward 1 for treated streets. |
| `rent_walkability_elasticity` | Land +30–50% within 50 m (2x city rate), with the premium spreading out to a ~500 m walkshed measured from access points. Supports a distance-decay with peak at frontage. |
| `ped_footfall_uplift` | 64,000 visitors/day on a new linear park. Destination-quality amenity creates new trips, not just diverted ones. |
| Transit | Subway downtown +4% to +14% after capacity removal. Mode shift goes mainly to rail when bus reform is concurrent. |
| Business churn | Net business count +3.5%, but losses in auto- and freight-dependent sectors. The sim's retail module should differentiate sectors (F&B/retail gain, wholesale/auto-service lose). |
