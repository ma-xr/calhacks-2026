# Oslo: "Bilfritt byliv" (Car-free Liveability Programme), 2016–2023

## Context
The 2015 city government pledged a car-free centre inside Ring 1. After business pushback, the plan shifted from banning cars to **removing on-street parking and re-routing through-traffic**, while keeping access to garages and deliveries ([Oslo kommune, "A step towards the city centre of the future 2017–2019"](https://www.oslo.kommune.no/get-file/1124386/7a686b533c0ff4dd2d99a24690810804503b2a805c36c5cc8b9345e6012a0a87)). This makes Oslo the cleanest analogue for an *access-preserving* FiDi plan.

## Caveats / confounds
- Toll-ring changes (2017, 2019) and residential parking happened at the same time. The traffic report says effects cannot be attributed to single measures. The −28% is an upper bound for what parking/circulation alone achieved.
- Counts are November snapshots plus radar validation, and the city-life counts are one-day snapshots.
- Retail outcomes are mixed: growth slowed into 2018, as it did nationally. The car-free area did not clearly outperform the rest of Oslo in 2018 (−2% vs +1%). The honest reading is "no measurable harm," not a demonstrated uplift.

## What our model should reproduce
| Parameter / output | Target |
|---|---|
| `traffic_evaporation_median` | Removing ~760 on-street spaces + blocking through-routes cut area car traffic by **−28% over 3 years** (−11% then −19%), with no change in driver destinations. That is genuine trip suppression/consolidation, not just re-routing. Use ~15–30% for an area-wide parking + circulation plan, scaled by private-garage supply (−36% where garages are few, −8% where they are many). |
| Parking elasticity | The effect is much larger where private off-street parking is scarce. FiDi has abundant garages, so expect the lower end (closer to −8%) unless garage access is also restricted. |
| `ped_footfall_uplift` | **+14%** people on streets over 2 years (area-wide, Gehl counts) |
| Staying / social ties | **+43%** people at leisure, roughly 3x the footfall uplift. This matches Copenhagen and NYC: dwell rises faster than flow. |
| `ped_street_retail_uplift` | About 0% net (range −3 to +6% relative to references). Shopping centres inside the area +6% in 2018. The sim should NOT promise large retail gains from parking removal alone; seating and programming drive dwell. |
| Freight | Van counts stable while cars fall. Keep delivery access in the sim, or freight costs/complaints will dominate. |
