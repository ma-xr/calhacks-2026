# Broadway Boulevard, Herald Square and Madison Square, NYC (2008–2010, plus Broadway Vision 2022–2025)

## Context
Broadway cuts diagonally across the Manhattan grid. Each crossing of a north-south avenue (Times Sq/7th Ave, Herald Sq/6th Ave, Madison Sq/5th Ave) created a complex multi-leg intersection with higher crash rates than other avenue intersections ([NYC DOT GLM Evaluation 2010, p.4](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/greenlight_midtown_nycdot.pdf)). DOT redesigned the corridor in stages: "Broadway Boulevard" (42nd–35th St, summer 2008: travel lanes narrowed from four to two, painted/gravel-surfaced pedestrian space) ([PBIC case study](https://www.pedbikeinfo.org/examples/example_details.cfm?id=4587)); Madison Square/Flatiron plaza expansion (2008) ([Streetsblog 2017](https://nyc.streetsblog.org/2017/03/29/dot-plans-to-turn-broadway-by-madison-square-into-a-shared-space/)); and the 2009 Green Light for Midtown closures at Times and Herald Squares.

## Caveats / confounds
- Short evaluation windows; the 2008–09 recession overlaps the study period; DOT retimed signals at the same time, so travel-time and injury changes cannot be attributed to pedestrian space alone.
- The large peak-hour percentages (+142%) come from single corners and are partly route shifting toward the new plazas, not new trips.
- The Madison Sq −29% crash figure is DOT-reported via the press; the underlying table was not located.
- Business outcomes are surveys, not sales data. NYC DOT sales-tax analyses ([Measuring the Street 2012](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/measuring_the_street_nyc_dot.pdf)) cover other plazas, not Herald or Madison Sq specifically.
- Broadway Vision surveys are self-selected BID respondents.

## What our model should reproduce
| Parameter / output | Target |
|---|---|
| `ped_footfall_uplift` (all-day) | +5% to +11% within 6 months on a closed diagonal through a dense CBD. Use about +6–8% as a FiDi central value (Market St / Kearny-type diagonals), since commuter corridors resemble Herald Sq more than tourist-heavy Times Sq. |
| Peak-hour footfall | Peak uplift larger than all-day (about +30% at Herald Sq) because crowding had suppressed peak demand. The sim should let sidewalk-capacity relief raise peak flows more than off-peak. |
| Spatial redistribution | Gains cluster at plaza edges (+45% to +142% at adjacent corners) and transit entrances near plazas gain share. Footfall shifts, not just grows. |
| Dwell / lingering | Stationary population +20% to +84%. Use this for the "social ties" / third-place output. |
| Transit speed | A parallel avenue that gains green time: bus speed +13%. Removing a diagonal leg can speed up crossing transit. |
| `traffic_evaporation_median` | Little net evaporation. Expect diversion to the nearest forced-turn streets (+5% to +11%) and slower travel on those links (up to −24%). |
| Safety | Pedestrian injuries −35% (corridor) to −53% (simplified intersection); crashes −29% at Madison Sq |
