# Case Study: Embarcadero Freeway removal (1989 → 1991) and The Embarcadero boulevard (2000)

**Relevance to our sim:** This happened *inside our bbox* (the Embarcadero edge of FiDi / SoMa). It is the canonical SF evidence that removing a high-volume road did not produce gridlock, and that land value and development responded strongly. It anchors the long-run land-use and City Value side of the model, not short-run traffic speeds.

| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|

## 3. Measured before/after

### 3a. Traffic and transport

| Metric | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Boulevard volume (c. 2000) | **~26,000 veh/day** (vs ~60,000 to 100,000+ on the freeway) | [Garrick & Billings](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/26135/dot_26135_DS1.pdf) |
| Gridlock | Did not materialise. Traffic was absorbed by other Bay Bridge ramps and the SoMa grid, helped by signal timing, restriping, one-way couplets and more transit | [Cervero et al. 2007](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5ps0v8d2/qt5ps0v8d2.pdf) |
| F-line / waterfront streetcar ridership | ~20,000 per day (2000) | [Garrick & Billings](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/26135/dot_26135_DS1.pdf) |
| Transit commute trips, impact zone, 1990→2000 | **+75%**. Transit gained mode share in the impact area while it fell in comparison areas | [Cervero et al. 2007](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5ps0v8d2/qt5ps0v8d2.pdf) |
| Walk-to-work share, 1990→2000 | +1.6 pp impact zone vs +1.0 pp comparison | same |
| Annual BART ridership | +15% (CNU; period and station unspecified, unverified) | [CNU](https://www.cnu.org/what-we-do/build-great-places/embarcadero-freeway-removal) |
| Injury accidents, 1 year after the quake (citywide, both freeways out) | Vehicle injury accidents +24%; pedestrian accidents −3% (SPUR 1990) | cited in [Cervero et al. 2007](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5ps0v8d2/qt5ps0v8d2.pdf) |
| Literature anchor used by Cervero | Goodwin et al. (1998), >100 capacity-reduction cases: average **−25%** motor traffic after accounting for parallel routes | cited in [Cervero et al. 2007](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5ps0v8d2/qt5ps0v8d2.pdf) |

How much of the freeway traffic "evaporated" versus diverted along the Embarcadero is **not quantified** in any source found (unverified).

| Metric | Impact zone | Comparison zone |
|---|---|---|
| Housing units, 1990→2000 | **+54%** | +31% |
| Jobs, 1990→2005 | **+23%** | +5.5% |
| Asian households, 1990s | +185% | declined |
| Chinatown employment (northern terminus) | fell ~1/3 (confounded by competing districts on Clement St and in Oakland) | — |

Hedonic model, residential sales 1986–2005, within 2 mi (Cervero et al. 2007, Table 1, 2007 USD, inflation-controlled):

| Effect | Coefficient |
|---|---|
| Sale while the freeway stood (1986 to Feb 1991) | **−$118,000** per unit (freeway dis-amenity) |
| Per foot of distance from the Embarcadero | −$64/ft (waterfront amenity) |
| Within ¾ mi of the corridor (full period) | −$213,621 (nuisance of a busy road) |
| Interaction: within ¾ mi × post-boulevard | **+$283,740** (offsetting amenity after June 2000) |
| Post-2000 general dummy | −$300,757 (dot-com bust confound) |

## 4. Caveats / confounds

- The dot-com boom (1995–2000) and bust (2001) dominate the price series. Cervero et al. flag the −$300k post-2000 dummy as a likely confound.
- The released 650,000 sq ft of land is a separate cause from the "amenity" effect. Regeneration may stem "as much" from parcel availability as from the roadway change (Cervero et al.).
- Pre-quake ADT is disputed (60k to 100k+). The earthquake closure acted as a two-year natural "pre-adaptation" period before demolition.

## 6. What our model should reproduce

| Target | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Treated corridor volume after conversion to boulevard | ~**−55% to −75%** (60k–100k → 26k veh/day) | Range reflects the disputed baseline |
| `traffic_evaporation_median` (cross-check) | Use the Goodwin et al. median of **~25%** network-wide reduction for capacity removal. Embarcadero is consistent with that and gives no tighter SF estimate | Cervero et al. cite Goodwin 1998 |
| No gridlock | The model must not produce sustained network collapse when an arterial is removed, provided the grid (SoMa) has parallel capacity and signals are retimed | qualitative, multiple sources |
| Transit response | Transit commute trips **+75%** in the impact zone over ~10 years (long-run, with a new streetcar). Do not apply in short-run sims | Cervero et al. |
| Land value (long run) | Freeway dis-amenity ≈ **−$118k/unit** removed; post-boulevard amenity ≈ **+$284k/unit within ¾ mi** (2007$). Our City Value should show a **positive, distance-decaying** property effect concentrated within ~¾ mi | Cervero et al. hedonic |
| Housing/jobs growth (decade) | Impact zone outgrew comparison by **+23 pp housing** (54 vs 31%) and **+17.5 pp jobs** (23 vs 5.5%) | Cervero et al. |
