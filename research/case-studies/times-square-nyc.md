# Times Square, NYC — "Green Light for Midtown" (2009)

## Context
Broadway's diagonal across the Manhattan grid created multi-legged intersections at Times Square (7th Ave) and Herald Square (6th Ave). Before the project, Broadway at Times Square carried ~1,400 veh/h ([NYC DOT, Green Light for Midtown Evaluation Report, Jan 2010, p.16](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/greenlight_midtown_nycdot.pdf)), while pedestrians were 77% of north-south travelers through Times Square ([same, p.23](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/greenlight_midtown_nycdot.pdf)). A 2009 DOT pilot deck reported only 11% of space allocated to pedestrians despite 4.5x as many people as vehicles (secondary summary via [PBIC](https://www.pedbikeinfo.org/examples/example_details.cfm?id=4587); primary deck not located, treat as (unverified)).

## Caveats / confounds
- Before/after windows are short (Apr vs Oct 2009 for counts; 6 months of injuries vs 3-year average). There was a recession in fall 2008–2009; DOT says itself it was "too soon to say with absolute certainty" there was positive economic influence (GLM report p.33).
- Speed gains are mostly taxi GPS; crosstown results were "highly variable"; DOT also retimed signals, so the speed effect is not from pedestrianization alone.
- Rent +180% and top-10 ranking coincide with a global tourism/flagship-retail boom; there is no counterfactual. The DOT sales data excludes chain stores and non-retail businesses ([WNYC](https://wnyc.org/story/285770-nyc-dot-study-street-redesign-good-for-the-economy/)).
- Times Square is a tourist destination, not a weekday office CBD. FiDi footfall is more commuter-driven.

## What our model should reproduce
| Parameter / output | Target from this case |
|---|---|
| `traffic_evaporation_median` | Network volume roughly unchanged (+3% entering the area) while the closed link's 1,400 vph moved to parallel avenues (7th +53%, 11th +22%). Treat it as low evaporation (~0–10%) when the closure is a short diagonal link and the grid has spare capacity. Most flow re-routes. |
| Parallel-street effect | Nearest parallel arterial absorbs the largest share (+50% on 7th Ave); farther ones see small or negative changes |
| Network speed | Small change in either direction (−2% to +17% by direction). The sim should NOT predict gridlock from closing a diagonal or redundant link. |
| `ped_footfall_uplift` | +6% to +17% through-volume in the first 6 months (+11% Times Sq, +6% Herald Sq); stationary/lingering population much larger (+84% avg, ~5x at one site) |
| `ped_street_retail_uplift` | DOT plaza sites: +14% (seating) to +172% (Pearl St) relative to borough +18%. Use a conservative central value of +10–30% relative to control, and note that the high values come from small bases. |
| `rent_walkability_elasticity` | Times Sq rents +180% (city claim, confounded). Do not calibrate an elasticity on this figure alone; use it only as an upper bound. |
| Safety | Pedestrian injuries −35% to −40%, all-mode −39% in treated area |
