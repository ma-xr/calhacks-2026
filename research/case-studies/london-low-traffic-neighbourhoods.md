# London Low Traffic Neighbourhoods (LTNs): Waltham Forest mini-Holland (2015–19) and 2020 emergency LTNs

## Context
Outer London boroughs Enfield, Kingston and Waltham Forest received "mini-Holland" funding. Only Waltham Forest built LTNs before 2020: residential cells where modal filters (bollards, planters, bus gates) stop through-traffic but keep every address reachable by car. In 2020, ~70+ emergency LTNs were added across London (count (unverified)). The evidence base is unusual: a longitudinal panel survey with controls (People and Places), plus STATS19 injury and police crime data analysed against Outer London controls. This is the best-identified quasi-experimental evidence in this folder.

## Caveats / confounds
- LTN samples in the panel are small (46–66 people), so CIs are wide. The response rate was ~1–2% (weighted).
- Injury analyses do not control for changes in exposure. The "3–4x safer per trip" figure combines counts with survey travel changes.
- LTNs are residential cells, not CBD streets. Car-ownership and walking effects reflect household behaviour, which matters less for FiDi commuters but applies to SoMa residents.
- Displacement medians from Thomas & Aldred are not verified in this file. Individual boundary roads can see increases even when the average does not.

## What our model should reproduce
| Parameter / output | Target |
|---|---|
| `traffic_evaporation_median` | Inside a filtered cell: motor trips −56% (first LTN). Boundary roads: approximately no change in injuries or crime, consistent with most removed traffic *not* reappearing on the boundary. Use LTN-interior evaporation of ~50% and boundary uplift near 0 (±10%) as default, with variance by road. |
| Walking response | +76 to +115 walking min/week per resident (≈+11 to +16 min/day). Use this for residents of pedestrianized SoMa blocks. |
| Car ownership | −20% (RR 0.80) after ~3 yrs; −7% household vehicles in crime study. Slow-moving variable: ramp over 3 years. |
| Social ties | Talking with neighbours locally +29% to +48% (RR 1.29–1.48). Directly usable for the social-ties delta. |
| Safety | All-mode injuries inside the zone ×0.31; per-trip risk ÷3–4; boundary ×~1.0 |
| Crime | Street crime −10% (−18% by year 3), no displacement. Bike theft rises (×1.85) from more bikes. |
| Emergency access | No measurable slowdown (±0 s). The sim should not penalize emergency response for filtered streets. |
