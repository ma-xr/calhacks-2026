# Strøget and the Copenhagen inner-city pedestrian network (1962–2005+)

## Context
Strøget is a ~1.1 km chain of medieval streets from City Hall Square to Kongens Nytorv ([Global Designing Cities Initiative, via Wikibooks casebook](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Transportation_Planning_Casebook/Stroget,_Copenhagen)). By the 1950s it was jammed with cars. Its conversion is the oldest long-running pedestrianization experiment in Europe, and Jan Gehl's team at the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts School of Architecture surveyed it in 1968, 1986, 1995 and 2005 (*Public Spaces – Public Life* 1996; *New City Life* 2006). It is the origin of the "public space – public life" counting method ([Gemzøe lecture, Gehl Architects](https://courses.washington.edu/gehlstud/gehl-studio/wp-content/uploads/documents/LarsGemzoe_Lecture_2015.pdf)).

## Caveats / confounds
- Survey methods changed over 40 years (snapshot counts on summer days, a different study area). The 350% / "4x" staying figures and the 35% first-year rise come from Gehl-affiliated or secondary sources, not a controlled evaluation.
- The headline result is about *staying*, not passing through. Through-movement on Strøget was already high in 1962. Growth in footfall came mainly from network expansion and a culture of outdoor cafés, alongside rising tourism and incomes.
- Copenhagen also built a cycling network, cut parking and grew transit at the same time, so car-free area is not the only driver.
- No modern retail-sales or traffic-displacement data exist for 1962. Do not cite Strøget for retail uplift or evaporation percentages.

## What our model should reproduce
| Parameter / output | Target |
|---|---|
| `ped_footfall_uplift` (short run) | About +35% pedestrian traffic in year 1 on the converted street (Gehl). This is an upper-range value for a flagship retail spine. |
| Mature pedestrian spine volume | Order of 3,000 peds/h midday (Strøget 2010) and ~80,000/day in summer. This is a sanity ceiling for a fully pedestrianized FiDi spine (Market St already carries comparable volumes). |
| Staying / social-ties output | Stationary activity scales about 1:1 with car-free area (≈4x area → ≈4x staying, 1968–2005), while through-movement grows much less. Our social-ties metric should respond to *dwell space*, not only footfall. |
| Café / outdoor seating | +50–60% per decade after conversion. Use this as a proxy for food-and-beverage frontage conversion over time. |
| Time dynamics | Effects build over decades through incremental expansion (1x → 3x area in 11 years, 6x in 34). The sim should show a ramp, not a one-off step. |
| What NOT to calibrate here | Retail uplift, traffic evaporation, `rent_walkability_elasticity`: no defensible Strøget numbers |
