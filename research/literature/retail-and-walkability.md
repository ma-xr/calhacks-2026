# Retail and Walkability: Literature Synthesis

Scope: what the evidence says about how pedestrianising streets and improving the public realm change footfall, spending, vacancy and rents, plus the behavioural parameters (visit frequency, ticket size, lunch/coffee habits, Huff decay) our shopping model needs.
Convention: every number has an inline link. "(unverified)" means we could not reach a primary or reliable secondary source. "(derived)" means our own arithmetic on cited inputs.

---

## 1. Key findings

**Systematic review.** [Volker & Handy 2021, *Transport Reviews* 41(4):401–431](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/transr/v41y2021i4p401-431.html) reviewed 23 US/Canada studies: 8 compared spending by mode and 15 measured business impacts, 10 of them with controls. Active-travel improvements had **positive or non-significant** effects on nearby retail and food service, with possible harm to auto-centric businesses. This is the most defensible single summary statement. [UC Davis record](https://itspubs.ucdavis.edu/publication_detail.php?id=3523)

**Walking distance responds to street design (San Francisco data).** Sidewalks about 3 m wider increase the distance people are willing to walk by about 84 m in SF. Traffic volume and speed each cut it by about 60 m (Sevtsuk et al. 2021; Basu & Sevtsuk 2022). [Pedestrian Pound 2024](https://reports.livingstreets.org.uk/pedestrianpound/MainReport/index.html); [Basu & Sevtsuk 2022, TR-A](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transa/v163y2022icp1-19.html)

### 1.4 Rents and property values

- [Pivo & Fisher 2011, *Real Estate Economics* 39(2)](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/the-walkability-premium-in-commercial-real-estate-investments/): a **+10-point Walk Score is associated with +1% to +9% value**, depending on property type. Office, retail and apartment show the effect; industrial does not. Sample: 4,200+ properties, 2001–2008. Walkability also brings lower cap rates and higher incomes, with no significant effect on total returns.
- Carmona/TfL 2018: retail rents **+7.5%**, office rents **+4%** after street improvements. [road.cc](https://road.cc/index.php/news/251992-more-shoppers-more-shops-tfl-stats-show-benefits-designing-streets-around)
- Rodriguez & Leinberger 2023 (35 largest US metros): the most walkable areas command **35–45% price premiums**. Note that this is a cross-section, not a causal estimate. [Pedestrian Pound 2024](https://reports.livingstreets.org.uk/pedestrianpound/MainReport/index.html)

### 1.5 Huff gravity model: distance decay

- ArcGIS Business Analyst: the uncalibrated default distance exponent is **β = 1.5** (attractiveness exponent 1). The original Huff distance exponent typically falls in **1.5–2**. Convenience goods such as groceries take larger β than comparison goods such as furniture. [Esri Pro docs](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/3.2/tool-reference/business-analyst/understanding-huff-model.htm); [Esri 10.2 docs](https://resources.arcgis.com/en/help/main/10.2/00mm/00mm0000004w000000.htm)
- Esri notes that **exponential** decay is typically used for short, within-city interactions. That suits walking. [Esri 10.2 docs](https://resources.arcgis.com/en/help/main/10.2/00mm/00mm0000004w000000.htm)
- A network-based Huff extension for pedestrians exists (Sevtsuk & Kalvo 2018, *Environment and Planning B* 45(3):508–528; no open link retrieved), but we could not retrieve its calibrated β. **A walking-specific β of 1.5–2.5 is unverified.**
- The average walk-trip length to Portland businesses was **0.7 mi**, versus 4.1 mi by auto. [Clifton et al.](https://carrefour.vivreenville.org/storage/app/media/publications/Articles-de-fond-Encyclopedique/infrastructure-cyclable/consumer-behavior-and-travel-choices-focus-cyclists-and-pedestrians.pdf). In Bristol, 42% of shoppers had travelled under 0.5 mi. [Cyclescape](https://richmondlcc.cyclescape.org/library/notes/94)

### 1.7 Office-worker lunch, coffee and near-office spending

- **Lunch out.** ezCater's 2025 Lunch Report (June 2025, n = 1,000 full-time on-site/hybrid US workers) found employees **buy lunch about 2.6×/week** and spend **$34.82/week** on bought lunches, up 26% YoY. Total lunch spend including packed lunches is $108.68/week. 51% skip lunch at least once a week. These figures come via secondary coverage and were not confirmed on ezCater's own page. [AOL/Moneywise](https://www.aol.com/articles/week-worth-lunches-now-cost-130000134.html); [BusinessWire ezCater release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251014181800/en)
  - (derived) 2.6 / 5 workdays is about **52% of in-office days** with a bought lunch. $34.82 / 2.6 is about **$13.40 per bought lunch**, a national figure.
- **Coffee out.** NCA NCDT Spring 2025: **66% of US adults drink coffee daily**. Among coffee drinkers, 71% drank only home-prepared coffee, **16% only out-of-home, 13% both**. [Convenience.org / NCA](https://www.convenience.org/stay-current/news/2025/april/16/5-average-american-has-3-cups-of-coffee_research); [search summary of NCA Spring 2025](https://ncausa.org/Newsroom/Grounds-for-celebration-Americans-remain-committed-to-coffee)
  - (derived) 0.66 × 0.29 is about **19% of adults per day** with an out-of-home coffee. Office commuters are plausibly higher (unverified).
- **Near-office spending lost to WFH.**
  - WFH Research estimates SF workers spend **$3,567/yr less** near work, tied to a 34.7% drop in in-person work. SF Standard scales this to about **$2.9B/yr** citywide. [SF Standard 2023](https://sfstandard.com/2023/02/20/san-francisco-is-losing-billions-a-year-in-local-spending-to-remote-working/)
  - A Bloomberg analysis of the same data gives **$3,040/yr per SF worker**, versus $4,661 in Manhattan. [Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/economy/remote-work-costs-nyc-over-12b-annually)
  - An alternative SWAA estimate is **$5,293** per SF worker. [Streetsense](https://streetsense.com/average-spending-by-office-workers-is-set-to-decline-by-as-much-as-50-heres-what-you-need-to-know/)
  - SF BLA: **~147,000 fewer office workers downtown per workday**. [SF BLA report 2023](https://sfbos.org/sites/default/files/BLA.DowntownSF.Economy%20Tax.022423.pdf)
  - **The pre-pandemic "~$15k/yr per office worker" figure is unverified.**

### 1.8 Value added share of retail and food sales

These figures convert revenue deltas into GDP-like "value".
- **Retail trade.** BEA value added was **$1,766B** in 2024 (avg of quarters; [FRED VAR](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/VAR)). Gross output was **$2,778.7–2,897.6B** across the 2024 quarters ([FRED GOR](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/GOR)).
  - (derived) VA/GO is about **0.62**. Note that retail gross output is the trade *margin*, not sales. VA as a share of total retail sales is much lower, roughly a quarter (unverified; it depends on the Census sales total).
- **Food services and drinking places.** 2024 VA (GDP) was **$703.1B** ([FRED USFOODDPNGSP](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/USFOODDPNGSP)). 2024 sales were about **$1.12T** (sum of monthly NSA values, [FRED RSFSDPN](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/RSFSDPN)). (derived) VA/sales is about **0.63**.

## 2. How our model uses it

| Parameter key | Recommended value (range) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `ped_street_retail_uplift` | **+15%** sales on pedestrianised frontage (range **0 to +30%**; outliers +49 to +172%) | Volker & Handy: positive or n.s. Living Streets "up to 30%". NYC FiDi Pearl St +14%. Shrewsbury +25%. Seoul +10–12%. We discount the NYC headline numbers (+172%, +49%) as selection-biased. |
| `ped_footfall_uplift` | **+25%** pedestrian volume (range **+18% to +93%**) | Lisbon +18%; London +93% walking (Carmona); "up to 40%" (Living Streets) |
| `walker_vs_driver_visit_ratio` | **2.0** (range 1.1–3.2) | SF downtown walkers 8 vs drivers ~4 visits/mo (2.0). Portland convenience 11 vs 9.9 (1.1). Portland restaurants transit ~8 vs auto 2.5 (3.2). |
| `avg_ticket_usd.cafe` | **$7** ($5–8) | Toast cold brew $5.58; SF latte $6.25–8 (unverified) |
| `avg_ticket_usd.restaurant` | **$28** ($20–35) | Toast FSR lunch check $31 (2023) |
| `avg_ticket_usd.fast_food` | **$13** ($10–15) | ezCater derived $13.40 per bought lunch; NPD 2013 floor $5.30–7.40 |
| `avg_ticket_usd.bar` | **$25** ($17–35) | Portland $17–22 in 2012$, inflated (assumption) |
| `avg_ticket_usd.shop` | **$45** ($40–88) | SF downtown 2007–08 per visit |
| `lunch_out_share_office_workers` | **0.52** of in-office days (range 0.4–0.7) | ezCater 2.6/5 |
| `coffee_trip_share` | **0.30** of in-office days (range 0.19–0.45) | NCA-derived 19% of all adults; we assume office commuters run higher (assumption) |
| `retail_value_added_share` | **0.60** of revenue for food service (range 0.25–0.63) | FRED-derived food-service VA/sales 0.63. Comparison retail is closer to 0.25 of sales (unverified). |
| `rent_walkability_elasticity` | **+0.5% value per Walk Score point** (range 0.1–0.9%/pt) | Pivo & Fisher: +1–9% per 10 pts |
| Huff β (internal) | power **β = 2.0** (1.5–2.5), or exponential decay over network metres | Esri default 1.5, typical 1.5–2. Walking-specific value unverified. |

**Mechanics.**
- Revenue = Σ visits × ticket. Visits come from Huff choice plus a walk-by exposure term.
- Pedestrianisation raises (a) street attractiveness, which acts through the footfall uplift and walk-by exposure, and (b) the visit frequency of agents who switch to walking, via the walker/driver visit ratio.
- `ped_street_retail_uplift` is used as a **validation target and cap**, not as an input. If emergent sales uplift on a pedestrianised segment exceeds about +30%, we flag it as outside the evidence band.

## 3. Limitations

- **Selection bias.** NYC DOT sites were chosen and reported by the implementing agency, and the comparisons are borough-wide rather than matched controls. The NYU Marron blog flags this ([Marron](https://marroninstitute.nyu.edu/blog/highlights-from-cityage-at-nyu)).
- **Mode effects are partly demographic.** Clifton finds that differences shrink once income, group size and frequency are controlled. Converting a driver into a walker does not automatically double their visits.
- **Displacement.** Most studies measure the treated street, not net city-wide gain. Some uplift is diverted from nearby streets, so the City Value should net this out or show it as an upper bound.
- **Post-COVID FiDi is unusual.** Office occupancy is low, so lunch and coffee demand scale with occupancy (see `office-to-residential-conversion.md`).
- **Ticket sizes mix years and places.** 2012 Portland, 2007 SF and 2013 NPD figures need inflation adjustment. The SF coffee prices come from guides.
- Not retrieved: the Auckland/Takapuna figures, the Graz merchant study, the full NYC DOT 2013 PDF (blocked) and the full TfL 2018 report (blocked).
