# Social Interaction in Public Space: Literature Synthesis

Scope: evidence that street design and traffic shape everyday encounters, that repeated co-presence turns into social ties, and what those ties are worth (wellbeing, mortality, money).
Convention: every number has an inline link. "(unverified)" means no primary or reliable secondary source was reached. "(derived)" means our own arithmetic.

---

## 1. Key findings

### 1.1 Traffic suppresses neighbouring (Appleyard and replications)

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Appleyard & Lintell (fieldwork ~1969; paper 1972; book *Livable Streets* 1981)**, San Francisco | 3 otherwise similar parallel residential streets (Franklin, Gough, Octavia) carrying **2,000 / 8,000 / 16,000 veh/day** ([Livable Streets & Social Capital summary](https://eionet.kormany.hu/akadalymentes/download/3/46/e2000/Livalble_Streets_and_Social_Capital.pdf); [PPS](https://pps.org/article/dappleyard)) | Light-street residents had **about 3 more friends and twice as many acquaintances** as heavy-street residents ([PPS](https://pps.org/article/dappleyard)). Per-resident means of **3.0 friends (light) vs 0.9 (heavy)** appear in a Streetfilms transcript ([Amara transcript](https://amara.org/videos/WrBmfcwZMu7b/en/103981)). **6.3 vs 3.1 acquaintances is unverified.** It is consistent with "twice as many" but we found no source. "Home territory" shrank as traffic grew. |
| **Hart & Parkhurst** (MSc 2008; journal version *World Transport Policy & Practice* 2011, journal details unverified), Bristol replication | 60 households on 3 streets with contrasting traffic | Friends and acquaintances were **significantly lower** on higher-traffic streets, and home territory shrank. Per-street means were not retrieved. [UWE repository](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/output/968892) |
| **Hounslow (London) residential street closure** (Aldred & Croft 2019) | Filtered permeability | Walking **+39%**, about 30% of it new trips. [Living Streets 2024](https://reports.livingstreets.org.uk/pedestrianpound/MainReport/index.html) |

### 1.2 Design drives optional and social activity (Jacobs, Gehl, Whyte)

- **Jacobs (1961)**, *Death and Life of Great American Cities*: "eyes on the street", short blocks, mixed primary uses and casual sidewalk contact build public trust. This is a qualitative foundation with no parameters.
- **Gehl**, *Life Between Buildings*: outdoor activities are necessary, optional or social. **Necessary activities are only weakly sensitive to quality.** **Optional activities occur only when conditions are good**, and social ("resultant") activities follow from both. When public space is poor, "only strictly necessary activities occur." [Uni-Weimar summary](https://otp.uni-weimar.de/forums/topic/functions-of-public-spaces-jan-gehls-three-types-of-outdoor-activities/); [book PDF](https://ebooks.umu.ac.ug/librarian/books-file/Life%20Between%20Buildings_%20Using%20P%20-%20Jan%20Gehl.pdf)
- **London high streets after improvements** (Carmona/TfL 2018): standing, sitting and waiting **nearly doubled**. This is a quantified Gehl effect. [road.cc](https://road.cc/index.php/news/251992-more-shoppers-more-shops-tfl-stats-show-benefits-designing-streets-around)
- **NYC FiDi Pearl St mini-plaza**: seated pedestrians **+77%**. [CBS NY](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/city-sponsored-study-touts-benefits-of-pedestrian-plazas-bike-lanes/)
- **Whyte (1980)**, *The Social Life of Small Urban Spaces*, Street Life Project for the NYC Planning Commission:
  - Sitting space is the dominant driver of plaza use. Sun, food and water matter less.
  - **"Triangulation"**: an external stimulus (performer, sculpture, food cart) prompts strangers to talk.
  - Corners host impromptu meetings, especially around 2 pm when lunch groups break up.
  - Seagram plaza was among the most used spaces.
  - [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Social_Life_of_Small_Urban_Spaces); [film transcript](https://amara.org/videos/dx1CGOMqY9eO/en). Specific Seagram head-counts are **unverified**.

### 1.3 Co-presence predicts ties

| Study | Data | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Eagle, Pentland & Lazer 2009**, *PNAS* 106(36):15274 | 94 subjects, about 330k hours of phone Bluetooth proximity, location and calls | **95% of (reciprocated) friendships inferred** from proximity plus calling patterns alone. Friend pairs have distinctive spatio-temporal proximity, for example off-hours and outside work. [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/55285); [Lazer page](https://northeastern.theopenscholar.com/david-lazer/publications/inferring-friendship-network-structure-using-mobile-phone-data) |
| **Copenhagen Networks Study** (Stopczynski et al. 2014, *PLOS ONE*; Sapiezynski et al. 2019, *Sci Data*) | **706** DTU first-years, 4 weeks, Bluetooth scans every **5 min** (8,064 bins), 6,429 Facebook edges | **>80% of texts** are exchanged within the **15% of dyads with the most proximity**. **89% of calling dyads** were co-present at least once. [Sapiezynski 2019 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6906316/); [Stopczynski 2014 (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4000208/) |
| **Kossinets & Watts 2006**, *Science* | 43,553 people at a university over 1 year of email; 22,611 students for focal analysis | "Focal closure": strangers sharing a class are much more likely to form a tie. A lecture summary puts it at **~140× without a mutual contact** (unverified against the paper). [Toronto lecture notes](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bor/303s20/docs/W4.pdf); [DOI record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/empirical-analysis-of-an-evolving-social-network) |
| **Hipp & Perrin 2009**, *City & Community* | New Urbanist neighbourhood | **Physical distance lowers the odds of both weak and strong ties.** Each extra month of co-residence raises ties. A **10% larger home-value gap is equivalent to 5.6% more physical distance** for tie formation. [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4tp2801z) |
| **Hall 2018/2019**, *J. Social & Personal Relationships* | 355 relocated adults + college freshmen | About **50 h** together to go from acquaintance to casual friend, **~90 h** to friend, **>200 h** to close friend. The adult-only figure is 94 h to casual friend. [Greater Good](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/how_long_does_it_take_to_make_a_friend); [Futurity/KU](https://www.futurity.org/friends-time-1718952-2/) |
| **Small 2009**, *Unanticipated Gains* | NYC childcare centres | Mothers using centres had **≥1 more good friend**. Centre friendships were associated with **>40% (poor) / ~60% (higher-income) lower odds of depression**. "Organisational brokerage" means routine co-presence plus inducements. [UChicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/child-care-pays-unexpected-dividends-parents); [Greater Good](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/how_childcare_boosts_social_capital/) |
| **Moro, Calacci, Dong & Pentland 2021**, *Nat. Commun.* 12:4633 ("Atlas of Inequality") | 4.5M phones, 1.1M places, 11 US metros | *Experienced* income segregation at places differs sharply from residential segregation, even at short distances. It is driven by "place exploration" and "social exploration". Places such as restaurants and shops have their own segregation level. [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/131211); [Oxford news](https://eng.ox.ac.uk/news/human-mobility-patterns-influence-how-we-experience-income-segregation-in-cities) |

### 1.4 Weak ties matter

- **Granovetter 1973**, *AJS*, "The Strength of Weak Ties". Among 54 Boston professionals who found jobs through contacts, the contact was seen **often 16.7%, occasionally 55.6%, rarely 27.8%** of the time. So about **84% came via weak ties**. [Wikipedia: Interpersonal ties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpersonal_ties); [AcaWiki](https://acawiki.org/The_strength_of_weak_ties)
- **Sandstrom & Dunn 2014**, *PSPB* 40(7):910–922. On days with more weak-tie interactions, people reported **higher wellbeing and belonging**. A brief friendly exchange with a barista raised positive affect versus an efficient one. The evidence is correlational plus a small experiment. [Essex repository](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/15300/); [summary PDF](https://www.goodmedicine.org.uk/sites/default/files/weak%20ties.pdf)
- **Mehl et al. 2010**, *Psych Sci* 21:539 (EAR recorder, n = 79, 4 days). Happiest vs unhappiest participants:
  - time alone **58.6% vs 76.8%**
  - time talking **39.7% vs 23.2%**
  - small talk **10.2% vs 28.3%**
  - substantive conversation **45.9% vs 21.8%**
  - [PMC2861779](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2861779). This cautions that not every encounter is equal: depth matters.
- **Klinenberg**, *Heat Wave* (2002) and *Palaces for the People* (2018). The 1995 Chicago heat wave caused **739** heat-related deaths. North Lawndale had a death rate about **10× that of Little Village**, even though the two neighbourhoods were demographically similar on paper. Klinenberg attributes the gap to street life, shops and "social infrastructure". [Prospect review](https://prospect.org/2002/10/09/books-review-d2/); [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/conversation-chicago-s-1995-heat-wave). Rates of **40 vs 4 per 100k are secondhand (unverified)**. Duneier 2006 critiques the individual-level inference. [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/ethnography-the-ecological-fallacy-and-the-1995-chicago-heat-wave/)

### 1.5 Health and mortality

- **Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton 2010**, *PLoS Med*: 148 studies, 308,849 participants. Stronger social relationships carry **OR = 1.50 (95% CI 1.42–1.59) for survival**. For complex social-integration measures, **OR = 1.91**. [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/c9833ba608474b048444b92ae7ee48ad); [BYU](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/7519)
- **US Surgeon General 2023 advisory**, *Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation*: lacking social connection raises premature-mortality risk **comparable to smoking up to 15 cigarettes/day**. It is associated with **+29% heart disease and +32% stroke** risk. About half of US adults report loneliness. [Decade of Healthy Ageing listing](https://www.decadeofhealthyageing.org/find-knowledge/resources/publications/u.s.-surgeon-general-s-advisory-our-epidemic-of-loneliness-and-isolation-and-the-healing-effects-of-social-connection-and-community); [NPR transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1173418268); [The Healthy](https://www.thehealthy.com/?p=1195008) (the 29/32% figures are secondary)

## 2. How our model uses it

| Parameter key | Recommended value (range) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `encounter_window_min` | **5 min** co-presence bins (range 5–15) | Copenhagen Networks Study 5-min Bluetooth bins. Eagle et al. likewise used phone-scan proximity. An encounter is two agents at the same place (venue, plaza cell, sidewalk segment) in the same bin, weighted by overlapping dwell. |
| `tie_formation_prob_per_repeat_encounter` | **0.03** per qualifying repeat encounter (range 0.01–0.10) | **Model calibration assumption, not a measured constant.** Hall: ~50 h to a casual friend, so weak ties (acquaintance, "nodding" level) need far less. We set p so that a resident on a calm street accrues roughly Appleyard's light-street acquaintance count (about 2× heavy) over a simulated year. Kossinets & Watts focal closure and Hipp & Perrin distance decay justify making p depend on shared venue, repeat count and proximity. Homophily: down-weight p for large income gaps (the Hipp & Perrin trade-off of 10% value gap ≈ 5.6% distance). |
| `value_of_weak_tie_usd_per_yr` | **$1,100/tie/yr** (range $300–$2,500) | (derived) HACT £4,511/yr for "regularly talks to neighbours" ÷ an assumed ~5 neighbourhood acquaintances ≈ £900 ≈ **$1,140**. Lower bound: spread across more ties, or discounted for causality. Upper bound: DCMS £9,537 for exiting severe loneliness, applied only to isolated agents. Cap per agent at the HACT total to avoid stacking. |

**Mechanics.**
- Pedestrianisation and plazas raise dwell time and optional activity (Gehl, Whyte, the TfL "nearly doubled" figure) and remove the traffic suppression Appleyard documented.
- More co-presence minutes in shared places produce more repeat encounters, then more weak ties, then wellbeing $ in City Value.
- Mortality effects (Holt-Lunstad) are **not** added on top of the wellbeing value. That would double count, since WELLBY valuations already include health top-ups in UKSVB v6.

**Validation targets.**
- The heavy-to-light-street acquaintance ratio should reproduce Appleyard's roughly 2×.
- On weekdays most new ties should form at lunch and coffee venues and at corners (Whyte).

## 3. Limitations

- **Causality.** Appleyard and Hart are 3-street cross-sections. Holt-Lunstad, Sandstrom and Helliwell are observational. Eagle and Copenhagen show *prediction*, not causation, and they use student or lab populations.
- **Appleyard's exact numbers** (3.0/0.9 friends, 6.3/3.1 acquaintances) could not be verified in the 1981 book. Use ratios, not absolutes.
- **Transfer of UK valuations to the US.** WELLBY and HACT are UK £ estimates for UK social-housing populations. Converting to USD (~1.27 $/£; assumption) ignores income and price differences.
- **Encounter quality.** Mehl shows small talk is not substantive conversation. Our encounter model counts co-presence, not depth.
- **Office districts.** FiDi encounters are mostly among co-workers and commuters, while residential neighbouring evidence is about neighbours. Office-to-residential conversion (see `office-to-residential-conversion.md`) moves FiDi closer to the setting the evidence describes.
- **Experienced segregation.** Moro et al. imply that new ties will be income-homophilous unless venues mix incomes. We should report tie diversity, not just counts.
