# Office-to-Residential Conversion in Downtown SF: Literature and Data Synthesis

Scope: current office vacancy and occupancy in FiDi and upper SoMa; SF's conversion policy stack; feasibility economics; units per square foot; and what conversion means for street life (residents create evening and weekend demand and neighbouring).
Convention: inline links for every number. "(unverified)" means no reliable source was reached. "(derived)" means our arithmetic.

---

### 1.5 Feasibility economics (SPUR / ULI / HR&A / Gensler 2023)

[HR&A feasibility study (Sept 2023)](https://www.spur.org/sites/default/files/2023-10/HR%26A_SF_Office_to_Residential_Conversion_Feasibility_Study_Sept_2023.pdf); [SPUR "From Workspace to Homebase"](https://www.spur.org/publications/research/2023-10-05/workspace-homebase)

- The study covers 6 typologies of underperforming CBD buildings (≥30% vacant), drawn from Gensler's review of 25 buildings. North and South FiDi hold **63%** of downtown office space.
- **Baseline result: conversion is not financially feasible** for any typology under 2022–23 market conditions.

| Typology (GSF) | Scenario 1 (status quo) gap/unit | Scenario 2 (office recovers) gap/unit | Scenario 3 (office declines further, resi improves) gain/unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 High-rise ≤12k sf floorplate (140k) | **−$75,000** | −$59,000 | +$140,000 |
| 2 High-rise 12–20k (290k) | **−$124,000** | −$115,000 | +$111,000 |
| 3 High-rise >20k (750k) | **−$79,000** | −$53,000 | +$113,000 |
| 4 Low/mid-rise 6–20k (100k) | **−$135,000** | −$121,000 | +$94,000 |
| 5 Low/mid-rise >20k (170k) | **−$121,000** | −$106,000 | +$99,000 |
| 6 Low/mid-rise <6k (20k) | **−$80,000** | −$26,000 | +$49,000 |

Key model assumptions:
- **650 net sf per unit**
- **efficiency factor 75–80%**
- **gross-to-rentable 90%**
- hard costs **$550–$730 per GSF**
- inclusionary fee $69/sf
- residential rent **$6.50–$7.25/sf/month**
- resi cap rate 4%; office B/C cap rate 7–8%
- 3 yrs to vacate plus 2 yrs to build
- The fee and inclusionary waivers since enacted (§1.4) target exactly the cost lines in this model.

### 1.6 Units per square foot (derived)

- HR&A: 650 nsf/unit ÷ (0.90 × 0.80) gives **~900 GSF per unit** (derived; range ~870–960 across typologies).
- City "30 by 30": 5M sf → ~5,000 units gives **~1,000 GSF per unit**, and 10,000 residents gives **~2.0 persons/unit**. [sf.gov 30x30](https://sf.gov/reports/july-2024/30-x-30)
- Real projects:
  - **100 Van Ness**, a 29-storey ex-CSAA office: **418 units** (completed; completion year ~2015 is unverified). [SCB](https://scb.com/2021/03/29/scbs-adaptive-re-use-of-100-van-ness-highlights-how-vacant-office-space-can-become-housing/); [Propmodo](https://propmodo.com/playbook/buildings/100-van-ness-avenue/)
  - **785 Market St** (19 floors): **124 units**. Started late 2024; later reports conflict on whether it was abandoned. [SF YIMBY](https://sfyimby.com/2024/10/office-to-housing-conversion-starts-at-785-market-street-san-francisco.html)
  - Pipeline (2026): **901 Market** (136 units), **2300 Stockton** (70 units), **150 Hayes** (104 units, Civic Center), **575 Sutter** (12 units). [Planetizen/Axios summary](https://www.planetizen.com/node/138001); [traded.co](https://traded.co/blog/emerald-fund-sdg-plan-104-unit-office-to-residential-conversion-in-san-francisco/); [SF YIMBY 575 Sutter](https://sfyimby.com/?p=47291)
  - **"140 Battery" as a notable conversion is unverified.** No source was found.

### 1.7 Downtown residential base today

- The Financial District neighbourhood shows a population of about **9,447** over 0.46 sq mi (Wikipedia infobox; year and source unclear, so unverified). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_District,_San_Francisco). Treat it as an order of magnitude only and replace it with ACS tract data (see `data-sources/`).

### 1.8 15-minute city implications

- Conversion adds residents who generate **evening and weekend** demand for cafés, groceries and services, smoothing the office district's lunch-only demand peak. This is consistent with the Roadmap's mixed-use goal. [Roadmap](https://www.sf.gov/news/mayor-breed-announces-roadmap-downtown-san-franciscos-future)
- WFH has cut near-office spending by about **$3,000–$3,600 per SF worker per year** (see `retail-and-walkability.md` §1.7). Every converted 1,000 sf of vacant office therefore swaps near-zero daytime demand for about 2 full-time residents.
- Residents are the population that neighbouring research describes (Appleyard; Hipp & Perrin; see `social-interaction-in-public-space.md`). Tie formation from co-presence applies more strongly to them than to commuters.

## 2. How our model uses it

| Parameter key | Recommended value (range) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `fidi_office_vacancy_rate` | **0.29** (range 0.26–0.31) | Kidder Q2 2026 FiDi 29.0%. CBRE Q1 2026 North FiDi 30.9%, South FiDi 26.1%. Use **0.33–0.35** for SoMa blocks (Kidder 33.2%, CBRE 35.4%). |
| `sf_office_occupancy_rate` | **0.47** of pre-pandemic attendance (range 0.44–0.55; peak days ~0.60+) | Kastle SF 47.2% (Oct 2026). Apply a day-of-week profile that peaks Tue–Wed. |
| Units per GSF (conversion block) | **1 unit per 900–1,000 GSF**; **2.0 residents/unit** | HR&A assumptions (derived); sf.gov 30x30 |
| Conversion feasibility flag (UI) | Show the 2023 HR&A gap of **−$53k to −$135k/unit** before the 2024–26 waivers. Show it as "likely closable" when Prop C, fee waivers and the financing district apply. | HR&A 2023; Bulletin 12 |
| `rent_walkability_elasticity` (cross-ref) | +0.5% value per Walk Score point | Pivo & Fisher 2011 (see retail file) |

**Mechanics.**
- Each office building's daily occupant count = rentable sf × (1 − vacancy) ÷ sf per worker × occupancy rate.
- Converting a block to residential removes those workers and adds residents = GSF ÷ 950 × 2.0.
- Residents get home-based activity schedules: morning coffee, evening and weekend shopping, and plaza time. This shifts the encounter and revenue time profile.

## 3. Limitations

- Brokerage vacancy figures differ by inventory and definition (Kidder 27.2% vs CBRE 30.4% vs JLL >30%). FiDi submarket boundaries differ between brokers.
- Kastle is a client-building sample and may understate trophy towers ([Planetizen](https://planetizen.com/node/119318)).
- The HR&A feasibility numbers use 2022–23 rents and costs. Since then SF apartment asking rents rose **14% from March to July 2026** ([SF OEA 2026 Q2](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/Status_of_the_San_Francisco_Economy_2026Q2.pdf)), and AI-driven leasing is cutting vacancy. Both shift the gap in opposite directions.
- Few downtown conversions have been completed, so units/GSF rests on assumptions plus a handful of projects.
- Downtown residential population is unverified at block level.
