# Traffic Evaporation and Induced Demand

Road capacity cuts and road capacity additions both change total vehicle travel. Classic static assignment assumes they don't. When a street is pedestrianized, some of its traffic diverts and some "evaporates": trips are re-timed, re-moded, re-destined, chained, or dropped. When capacity is added, the reverse happens.

**On terminology.** Cairns et al. (2002) are explicit that "disappearing" traffic is not physically vanishing. It is the net of behavioural responses: route change over a wider area, re-timing, mode shift, destination change, trip chaining, reduced trip frequency, and longer-run relocation. They describe a three-level model: perceived-only capacity loss, loss offset by spare capacity elsewhere, and real loss leading to behaviour change. "Evaporation" is shorthand for the share of former traffic that is **not found on the monitored screenline**, so it depends on how wide the screenline is ([Cairns et al. 2002](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/disappearing_traffic_cairns.pdf)).

## 2. How our model uses it

| Parameter key | Value we adopt | Range for sensitivity | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| `traffic_evaporation_median` | **0.106** (fraction of the pedestrianized street's prior car traffic not found on the surrounding network) | 0.05 (bus-lane-like) to 0.22 (mean); central-shopping median 0.137 | [Cairns et al. 2002](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/disappearing_traffic_cairns.pdf) |
| `car_trip_time_elasticity` | **−0.5** (short-run traffic volume w.r.t. travel time) | −0.2 to −1.0 (long run) | [Goodwin 1996](https://trid.trb.org/View/458127); also see `mode-choice-and-elasticities.md` |

**Use as a validation target, not a hard-coded rule.** Our sim does not delete trips by fiat. Evaporation should *emerge* from the behavioural layers:

1. **Route choice** on the BPR-congested graph across replanning "settle days". This captures the diversion part.
2. **Mode-choice logit**: car utility falls as congested time rises, so some agents shift to BART, Muni, or walking.
3. **Huff destination choice** for shopping: lower car access to a pedestrianized retail block reduces car-borne visits but raises walk-borne visits.
4. Optional **trip suppression or chaining** for discretionary trips.

**Calibration check.** After N settle days, compute Δ(car-km or car trips crossing the screenline around the treated street) ÷ (baseline car volume on the treated street). The result should land in roughly **−5% to −22%**, with a median-like target of about **−11%**. If our sim shows **0%** (pure diversion), the behavioural layers are too stiff. If it shows **>50%** on a single-street closure, they are too elastic.

**Induced-demand symmetry.** If a user *adds* capacity (for example, re-opens a street), long-run car VKT on that link class should rise roughly in proportion to lane-km (elasticity ≈ 1.0; [Duranton & Turner 2011](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15376.pdf)). Our study area is small and bounded by fixed I-80 and Bay Bridge gateway demand, so we expect a **smaller** response than the metro-wide elasticity. That figure includes migration and freight, which we do not model.

**Boundary-road check.** For multi-street pedestrianization (LTN-like cells), boundary-road volume change should be small relative to boundary-road base volume (London: <1% mean; [Thomas & Aldred 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cstp.2023.101124)). Large displacement in our sim is a red flag.

## 3. Limitations

- **Selection and publication bias.** Cairns' cases are schemes that were implemented and monitored. Schemes that caused chaos may be under-represented; the authors acknowledge "some tendency for a self-selection" ([Cairns et al. 2002](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/disappearing_traffic_cairns.pdf)).
- **Screenline dependence.** A narrow monitoring area inflates "evaporation", because traffic simply diverted beyond the counters. Our FiDi/SoMa box is small, so through-traffic diverted to the Embarcadero, or to 5th St and beyond, may leave the box. We should measure evaporation over the **whole simulated network plus gateway counts**, not only the adjacent blocks.
- **Mostly UK and European contexts.** Transit availability and car ownership differ. That said, downtown SF has high transit and walk shares, closer to European CBDs than to typical US contexts.
- **Context dependence of the elasticities.** Goodwin's −0.5/−1.0 are averages; he stresses dependence on scheme "context, size and location" ([TRID abstract](https://trid.trb.org/View/458127)).
- **Duranton & Turner** is a metro-scale interstate result. It is not directly transferable to a single downtown street.
- **COVID-era confounding** affects the London LTN study and the Barcelona study.
- **Time horizon.** Our settle days represent a few weeks of adaptation. Long-run responses (residential and job relocation, car ownership) are out of scope, so our evaporation should sit at or below long-run empirical values.
